Happy [Pi day][1]! I'm more of a fan of [tau][2] but not here to slice things
up. This week was met with a lot of reading; a return to [Livtet][] and more
planning for the rest of the year.

# Writing
In lieu of the current focus of Canadian sovereignty under supposed threat by
the current president of the United States, [I wrote a short note][blog:1]
about the nature of most colonial countries and the want for a broader lens in
what's really going on. The sense of panic is unique in that it's afforded to a
country that would _never_ see itself ever being subject to some sort of
foreign rule since it's done its time as a fellow imperial power. Something
about leopards and faces comes to mind.

I wrote something short about [the new direction I'm taking Livtet in][blog:2].
What I haven't mentioned but hinted at in that post is that I do want to make
this into something folks can use for self-hosting and using to share their own
libraries. I'm not sure if I'll host a service for the such but I do want to
use this to add more options for folks in the social Web to share things that
_isn't_ just microblogging &mdash; we have so many things we can work with.
This is my contribution!

# Reading
I finished [World Wide Waste][www] on Monday and wrote [my closing thoughts][3]
on the book on Bookwyrm. Some points were made in the book but it yoyo'd
between blaming pollution and waste _solely_ on consumers and then on companies
themselves. I have a physical book and I have to say, it was _hard_ reading the
book. Without explicit markers for footnotes, I didn't know if I was reading
the opinions of the author or if they were quotes &mdash; the extremely reduced
use of typographical hints had me read each page about twice and open the
associated links on my phone or browser to see what parts were quoted. You have
to have _time_ to read this kind of book and it's part of the reason why I
avoided finishing it for some time. I want that time back. It is interesting to
see the suggestions in this book be a bit of a soft retraction of things
mentioned in [an earlier book of McGovern][ce] from 1999 &mdash; what really
provoked that?

I've been enjoying [Lurking][]; a read on the evolution of folks' evolving
relationship with the Internet. Some of it is before my time and exposure to
the Internet but seeing the window at which they begin to overlap has given me
some newfound understanding of how folks older than me (and more invested in
the Web) choose to go about it. There's things in another book, [Black
Software][bs], mentions but I was a bit sad to see the omission of some of the
older Black social networks like AfroNet and the blogs. I think this is an
attribute of the nascent nature of social networking history (it's still pretty
novel of a field of research; ~30 years); but the notable ones like Black
Planet were up there.

Other books that I'm reading slowly are [World Eaters][book:1], [The Spear Cuts
Through the Water][book:2], [Distributed Blackness][book:4] and [Haiti: State
Against Nation][book:3] when time permits during the week. I'm very eager to
get deeper into Trouillot's works; I was inspired as I'm looking into the role
that faith had in the Haitian Revolution[^1].
