The month's coming to a close with four days left. I've all but finished my reading list for Black
August, while managing to make some progress again with Livtet and picked up work again on Shock.
I've added a new part to my site as well to [document links I've been reading][2].

# Reading
I'm realizing that, unless I have nothing else to do with my life (which is not likely), I won't
finish the reading list I set out for Black August. I'm not disappointed, in fact, I'm very glad
with the progress I've made of 4 out of 6 books for this month. Six was already a very high goal and
I'm proud that I got this far.

## [We Do This 'Til We Free Us][book:1] by Mariame Kaba
This book has been a roller-coaster. There's _so many_ things I want to quote from this but I'll
hold off until I finish [Livtet][3] so I can add more context. Kaba's interviews are a treasure
because both the interviewers and her responses have expanded so much (and left me with even more
questions on how I view prison abolition). It becomes more and more difficult to accept the
mainstream idea that we need to punish people in a way that does nothing but expand the harms we
claim to be against even more so.

You can view a lot of my thoughts (as well as others in the Fediverse) on the book so far over on [Bookwyrm][book:1.1].

## [Harlem Shuffle][book:2] by Colson Whitehead
Listen, I am a fan of this man! I've read [Apex Hides the Hurt][book:2.1] by him and I _loved_ the
way he was able to talk such a difficult topic (integration of a Black and white neighborhood) with
the overlay of branding. In Harlem Shuffle, he manages to do the same but with suspense. I am _a
bit_ disappointed with the ending but I understand why he did it that way. It relies a lot on a form
of storytelling that's intrinsic to African American communities: that of the past to help design
the future. I'm really glad that I got the ebook and audiobook combination for this; I imagine this
kind of book being adaptable to some limited television series would go over pretty well.

## [You Exist Too Much][book:3] by Zaina Arafat
This book threatened to make me cry and it almost succeeded, ha. The way Arafat closed the book was
necessary and so healing &mdash; the need to understand _where_ our definition of love comes from
and how to evolve from it when it no longer serves us. Having to deal with family that refuses your
identity in a country that refuses your identity is a double whammy that Arafat handles
_beautifully_ in this book; albeit in a drunken splendor.

## [None but the Righteous][book:4] by Chantal James
This book is _very_ different from ones I've read before. My knowledge of the South is limited and
I've been working to expand that. James' approach to narrating this story; through the lens of a
spirit is said to invoke the imagery of Toni Morrison's Beloved in a review. I haven't read anything
from Morrison outside of [The Source of Self Regard][book:4.1] so this is both a learning exercise
and a journey for me. Nonetheless, I am enjoying the story and eager to see Ham find what he's
looking for (if he even knows what that is himself).

# Writing
I wrote a longer piece about attempting to figure out how to engage people without holding
resentment for the world; wrestling with [the wrong ideas][writing:1]. I have yet to go through
feedback for this post but it's something I'm hoping to routinely expand on. I've learned that a
core component of being an activist - of any kind - is having a practice of self-reflection. I'm not
the best at that.

Additionally, I've found myself wanting to write about links or essays I read in print or online and
want to expand on what makes them interesting to me. I've written about [adding
bookmarks][writing:2] back to my site, in an IndieWeb fashion. Some things I've written about were
things in [_DISSENT_][writing:2.1], like [the right to strike][writing:2.2] or in [Fifth
Estate][writing:2.3] about [pacifism and anarchy][writing:2.4] in relation to war.

## Coding
I got a bit eager to work on [Shock][coding:1] because I became _immediately_ annoyed with how
writing bookmarks in this very manual fashion. Sure, it effectively serves as a bulk ward to
prevent me from "vomiting" on the Internet. It also slows down my ability to build out my digital
garden of the things I'm exploring. Hence my slow rebooting of the project &mdash; for the fifth
time. I'll write more about what's going on with it, but ideally with something more unique to it.

[Livtet][coding:2] is still seeing work. I've done a bit of a refactor on how it handles books to
focus on them being stored _only_ on disk for now. I didn't see a good way, at the moment, to handle
manipulation of libraries from the application. What I'm focusing on now is allowing for folks to
connect to remote libraries from their local libraries and keeping those books linked. I aim to have
something about this written out later this week.
