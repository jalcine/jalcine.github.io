## Reading
I got [Bitter Root v3][1] and [Black Science v5][2]. I haven't started either yet as I was focused on my current reading stack. 
Ideally, I'll do that this week.

[The Victorian Internet][3] was continued last week. Last week, I mentioned the role that Asiatic resources played into
the development of the underwater cable. By now, I've gotten to the point where it was successfully built. It came very
expensively. A person who resembles a white South African man that managed to tank the value of a social network managed
to do nearly the same to the early crafting of the first Trans-Atlantic cable. The initial release was so bad and so
frayed that "the official opening of the cable to public traffic was delayed again and again, and commercial messages
started to pile up at both ends, while the true state of affairs was kept under wraps. The reliability of the cable
steadily deteriorated, and it eventually stopped working altogether on September 1, less than a month after its
completion" in 1858. I _wish_ I had the level of disregard for society that Edward Whitehouse had - dude went to court
and "as he attempted to defend himself and his flawed theories, Whitehouse blamed everyone around him. Portraying
himself as a man of science struggling against the forces of ignorance and incompetence, he blamed the manufacturers of
the cable, the crew of the ship that laid it and most of all, [Cyrus Field][15] and the other officials of the [Atlantic
Telegraph Company][14], who he claimed had refused to let him carry out all the tests he recommended. Built different, I
say 🤣!

I've mentioned this in passing but I'm reading [Manufacturing Consent][4]. It's been taking me some time to read this, I
do find it harder to parse books like this - a semi-narrative retelling and journalistic expansion of events. While
reading this, I definitely made the connection this author is also the _same_ computer linguistics guy. Talk about using
your time productively, I guess? I'm a bit over halfway through the digital copy I'm reading. To be honest, I'm glad I
read this digitally because searching for the references mentioned has prevented me from getting too distracted. It's also no
surprise to me that the [Heritage Foundation][10], the same one behind the policy book, [Mandate for Leadership: A
Conservative Promise][11] _are also the same ones_ who pushed for Congress to "buy the top academic reputations in the
country to add credibility to corporate studies and give business a stronger voice on the campuses", quoted from the
book. Chomsky notes this as formulaic of right-wing groups since the 1970s, potentially a response to the rise of
revolutionary academics and rising student protests during the time. He writes, "the corporate funding and clear
ideological purpose in the overall effort had no discernible effect on the credibility of the intellectuals so
mobilized; on the contrary, the funding and pushing of their ideas catapulted them into the press". We see this with
large media outlets like the New York Times[^2]. I'm eager to finish this book but I'm taking my time.

I don't think I'll ever stop talking about [The Source of Self Regard][16]. Morrison is a true American writer. That'll
ruffle the imagination of those who fixate on Twain and the like but I make this point because she _writes_ about
America in a way that few can touch and still manages to be clear and direct. She writes, "there is a clear flight from
Blackness in a great deal of Afro-American literature", which is clear from the many media portrayals of Black people and
the narrative of representation in spaces where it devolves slowly into how much capital (social, fiscal or otherwise)
one has managed to accrue. It's rarely been about uplifting tangible community and is conventionally about transforming
people into subscribers or assets. [Spill][17] is a great example of this. They have the explicit goal of selling
people's content to advertisers who can optimize on knowing [it's a platform of Black folks][18]. Companies can visit this
platform, as demanded by venture capitalists, to optimize their traffic, at the behest of two Black folks running the
helm. I don't fault anyone for making money in a capitalist society but we can't be doing it at the cost of the privacy
of our own people. Let Zuckerberg do that. Morrison doesn't make any explicit demands - at least not to the reader. The
book begins with essays she's given to many organizations and graduating classes. I can't recommend it enough and I'm
only halfway through the book.

## Coding
So I mentioned, after a few days of messing around, that I'm working on something [a bit silly][19]. I wrote about parts
of it with [a small intro](/essays/my-hub) but I'd like to expand a bit here. The goal of <strong>livtet</strong>[^3] is
to make something I can use to actually host next to this website, link things I've read and serve as a personal archive
for my notes. Chris Ferdinandi's newsletter about ADHD is something that makes me think about needing this a lot, namely
[the need to dump][20] everything[^4]. This is one way for me to do that. It's going to be written in Rust using Tauri
as I've been using that for a lot for things like [Shock](/projects/shock) and other toy applications[^5]. Feel free to
drop any issues at <https://todo.sr.ht/~jacky/livtet> or send an e-mail to <~jacky/livtet@todo.sr.ht>, no account
required.

I've slowed down a bit on my work with Shock. The _guts_ of it is feature-complete for me. I need to figure out how to
handle sharing data between two clients. I've been wrestling with Veilid with no progress and I decided to examine using
librecast with a custom RPC layer to handle sync. We'll see how far I get with that.
