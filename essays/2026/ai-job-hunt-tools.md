I've spent a good amount of (virtual) ink on this site [being suspicious of
generative AI][1] and [arguing that protesting it is too easy][2] when the
actual power sits with capital allotment towards its development and research,
not with the model weights. I stand by that. I also [admitted a few weeks
ago][3] that I broke my own stance and started using it to follow the industry,
in hopes of improving my chances at getting a job at all. It's no different
than me returning to use GitHub for all of my project development, despite also
using things like <https://tangled.org> to begin hosting my code (as confusing
as it can be at times) and exploring non-Git tools like (not but sponsored
heavily by) Google's `jj`.

## The tools

[Jack and Jill][4] has been doing _some_ of the job-discovery legwork lately
&mdash; surfacing roles I wouldn't have found by manually refreshing the same
three job boards every morning, and helping tailor cover letters per role
instead of me copy-pasting the same paragraph with the company name swapped
out[^1]. I won't pretend the cover letters it produces are precious; they're
serviceable, which is exactly what a cover letter needs to be when you're
sending out five a day. I also am on LinkedIn _a lot_ - I yap about it and the
bugs I find (which were, ironically a result of uBlock Origin) and pipe roles
into Jack to help keep a second place to track all of my applications (I keep a
separate spreadsheet on my computer for my own quick math).

The other half of this is local: I use things like OpenCode, Pi and Claude Code
against [my own resume][5] to tailor it per role, when necessary. Since my
resume's yet another template that lives in this site's repo; that makes the
act of tweaking things _very_ quick and cheap. Having an LLM read a job posting
and my actual work history side by side and tell me which skills to
foreground (or which experience bullet is dead weight for this particular
application) is a much better use of my time. I was doing this manually and was
only able to get about 6 applications a day in when doing longer reviews. Now
it's _at least_ three times that.

## "Damn, son, now AI helping you look for work?"

None of this resolves the argument I made in [the ease-of-protest piece][2]:
the workers who build these tools still work for the people I have every
reason to distrust, and the fact that a consumer-facing product happened to
be useful to me this month **doesn't make the incentives underneath it any
better**. Like I mentioned above, this is mainly about increasing the number of
shots I can take in an industry that's already invested heavily into these tools.
What it does mean is that under enough precarity, the distance between
"principled abstention" and "can't afford to lose another week" collapses fast.

I don't know yet if any of this moves the needle in a market where [referrals
matter more than the work itself][3]. But it's cut down the time between
"found a posting" and "applied" enough that I can throw a wider net without
burning out on the process before lunch.
