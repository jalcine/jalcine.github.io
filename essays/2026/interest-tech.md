title: "Tech doesn't 'spark joy' the same way anymore."
---

Since my last (incomplete!) entry on holding [a leftist position on AI][1],
I've struggled to find interest in recent developments in the industry at
large. A lot of it seems to be comparable to some infection of the mind and
perception of what it means to do _anything_ with code and looking to replace
the developer as much as possible. Folks who declare good intents are tapping
the gas and are driving into the town of generative AI &mdash; seemingly as a
means of survival and relevancy. It's not terribly surprising as it reminds me
of the way that folks have latched onto things like React and Tailwind due to
its initial hype and the amount of people who have invested heavily into making
the framework part of their identity[^1]. Now, it's folks clammoring over
whispers over a new model release in ways I haven't seen since high school
&mdash; watching friends eager for sneaker drops or a video game release
outside of GameStop. We've &mdash; as an industry &mdash; have completed our
turn of obsession from making things for poeple to making things for businesses
_regardless_ of the harms or impacts it causes on people. It's getting
_weird_!

I recall a brief exchange with the lead organizer of RenderATL and their
will contribute to them becoming non-compeititve in the future. I do wish folks
in tech took some of their (generous) paid time off to read literature from
tech workers that came before them about the entreprenuer-centric language of
"worker obsolescent" innovation and who it tends to impact the most. It would
have also been a bit embarrassing, as a Black person promoting an ambigiously
Black tech event while taking in money from organizations who explicitly are
exploiting kinfolk for profit. This requires a truce - if not a complete end -
to the "diaspora wars" and a decentering of American technologists as the
epicenter of said industry but this isn't the kind of thinking that fits in
tech spaces. We can only do what Mark Zuckerburg has taught us and what we
choose to continue despite mantras otherwise &mdash; to move fast and break
things. AI-driven technologists can't engage ethics earnestly without
reflection and the only time we give ourselves to reflect is during sprint
retrospectives. I might be expecting too much from people. I remember an
exchange with a former friend who worked at Facebook that took _personal_
offense when I mentioned my disdain for the platform. It fulfilled the
stereotype of FAANG employees who see their employement as an extension of
their identity (and to a degree, their nationality - for a later conversation).
They no longer work at Facebook, for what it's worth, but since then have
doubled down in what DuBois was once part of - the ideology of the "Talented
Tenth" of the Black community; the people meant to shepard the rest of the
Black community from obscurity and into prosperity. A casual reader of world
affairs will note that this logic has only resulted in a very small and
non-representative demographic of Black people to gain visiblity and mine the
remainder of the community for culture and data points[^2].

There's a bit of hyprocrisy here that I'll admit [and highlight from my last
entry][1]. I do make use of generative coding tools - at work and in personal
projects like the rewrite of [Livtet][]. I don't make use of Amazon or
Microsoft's tooling for personal work but Microsoft's stack is the dominant
tool as they own and run a lot of government service software (if not Amazon)
and in prior work, it was more suggested than demanded to make use of Copilot.
People have to justify the purchase of these enterprise seats brought on by
executives who have a reputation for putting marginal profit over anything -
everything - else. In personal attempts, I flip between using things like
OpenRouter and OpenCode and found myself being dramatically slowed down in most
places with some "speed ups" in others. At times, the thing that I find the
most useful about this project is how it's centered documentation (back) in my
life as I work. I do it for my own form of task management as a form of
README-driven development but these "agentic" tools require puppet masters to
get to the level of usability that other folks are clamoring about from other
places.

It's completely possible that I am not wired or do work in the way where
"agentic" coding would be helpful. I'm not a 10x developer, I don't have big
notable projects &mdash; I like coding but I no longer have this yearn that's
slightly projected as a requirement in the space to "make it". These tools tend
to be very allegeric to this concept despite scraping _everything_ it can get
its hands on. And now that it's _extremely_ hard to avoid in mainstream
tooling; the once-soft social conditioning that was visible with React and
Tailwind (and to a degree, Visual Studio Code) has now reached my last place of
safety: my mind.
