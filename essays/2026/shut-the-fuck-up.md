Things are _always_ greener and simpler as they seem from the other side. I'm
working on my own project that I decided to get _way more serious_ &mdash;
namely around [Livtet][1], now a part of [my software lab][2] and a lot of
things that I used to chide _a lot_ of folks about. From running companies to
handling software, I am learning &mdash; in my big age of 33, that a lot of
shit is more complicated than it seems and hiding this complexity is 80% of the
job. The rest 20% is making sure the intended work and objectives are aligned.
I regret some of the dogpiling I (willingly!) participated in when it came to
the Fediverse and Atmosphere alike. Layfolk who haven't spent time working on
things only see the finished results and complain instead of producing feedback
that can be constructive.

I recently bemoaned the stress of wanting to run the social network that I plan
to launch with Livtet[^1] because if I do it the way I want to - a healthy,
safe and productive ecosystem - I _have_ to have durable moderation. I did a
bit of it when working with [PlayVicious, a nascent Black-owned and -run
Mastodon instance][3]. I hated the work and I was _barely_ doing it. It's
important work but it wasn't the kind of work that was the best use of my
skills[^2]. I am a software developer by trade and hobby and deeply, I'm a
tinkerer. I have experience with government, enterprise and retail software
systems but I still want to run around [a Framedeck][4] that collects data that
I can query when I go about my day instead of pulling out my laptop &mdash; I
have a small rig at home.

This isn't necessarily a full-on apology for my actions because what's done is
done and those bridges look slightly burned and I much rather leave them alone
but this blog post is a bit of a reminder and call-in-to-myself to _shut the
fuck up_ and see what's actually going on versus jumping on reactionary
critique. I've read way too much to be moving the way I do &mdash; I'm getting
back to it anyhow.
