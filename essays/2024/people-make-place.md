Since the 2024 United States election, we've all noticed a _dramatic_ shift in our social feeds.
While I still subscribe and am backlogged to many feeds[^1]; I still jump over and spend at least an
cumulative hour throughout the day on social media. Seeing the number of folks who've either made an
account or returned to their accounts on places like BlueSky or within the Fediverse has been
comforting. It's evidence that the adage "if you build it, they will come"[^2] can come to fruition.
My attention wobbles between Mastodon, where I'm more lurking and BlueSky, where I find myself
engaging more. The difference? [Rudy Fraser][rf]'s work with BlackSky.

## BlackSky is a Digital "Green Book"
Rudy's quick deployment around a customizable feed that presents Black people (and _solely_
them) is one that's tied with a lot of human moderation and the use of a core feature of what
ATProto provides: custom feeds. A custom feed, in other words, would be the equivalent of using a
list of people you follow to curate a timeline (as is the case with Mastodon and its clients') but
done externally of your own Mastodon account. This has multiple applications within the ATProto
ecosystem[^3]. This feed, the "BlackSky" feed, provides an expericene that reminds me of the times
I've spent on Twitter from 2015 up to the Acquisition. That provided me a bit of a reprieve from the
more mundane or cynical aspects of the timeline - of which I contribute to.

There's moments that I find that I would _not_ want to [backfeed][] to my website. I use my personal
website to write things like this and it's associated to quite a bit that I do online[^5] &mdash; to
a point of it being the content that people use in the LLMs without consent to define me.

## What _Could_ The Fediverse Try?
There _are_ ways to replicate this within the Fediverse. One could ask if a one-to-one replication
is merited: I'd say no. It's not that the Fediverse _needs_ it; it could definitely implement this.
But the chokehold that Mastodon has on the _concept_ of the Fediverse, in both of its
utilization and the team's cautious approach to feature expansion makes it unlikely without a pincor
move from the greater community.

At this point, I don't think there's a concrete technical solution for this. I was asked once about
the notion of bringing folks who tend to have a larger Internet presence to the Fediverse. I was
concerned about it for a few reasons: passive gamification of the people on the platform, deepening
the current legitimacy of celebrity-centric social network development. That tends to be a more
extreme version of what I'd like to see more of: a systems-friendly way to support artists and
creatives online. I was briefly into the idea of [Web Monetization][1] as a way to pull us away from
entities like Patreon that work as a nozzle over how we support people online.
