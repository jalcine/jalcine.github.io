I'm working on what I want this website to be like. I've stopped imagining it as some bastion of a
"quantified digital self", as difficult as that's showing to be since blogs and the Web are designed
to [be uniform in its chronological progression][1]. Additionally, I find that to be the de-facto
orientation and design of the Web as outlined and commanded by [Google since 1999][2], anything
that's not meant to be commercially beneficial falls back to the obsession of chronological sorting.
It's not a golden rule &mdash; a means of breaking away from that is by allowing an alternate algorithm
create a different kind of river of content that you can then exchange for hours of your life in
idle motion. However, [this requires a bit of effort][7].

A set of people who benefit from the idle (or rigorous, in opposition to their own desires) motion
of others tend to not be impacted as heavily by the majority of those who use the Web as often, in
exchange for their display of "disconnection"; something assigned for the rest of the connected
world to be in constant consumption of. These folks collect the capital produced by or in the
service of maintaining working on the technologies that folks come to loathe[^1]. It'll be used to
bolster their stances in
whatever spaces they deem relevant and run forward into the cliche American life of security and
comfort[^2]; now exportable around the globe. I've had the
unfortunate pleasure of being squished extremely close to folks like this. The things they'd focus
on are largely disconnected from the folks whom they extract value from. One thing that they avoid
is the concrete conversation around the political nature of what even enabled their rise to
success[^3]. I've been even informed to avoid the topics, of politics and money, altogether &mdash;
with no real reason by these exact people. The idea of talking about it would place them into the
antithesis of what they assume themselves to be &mdash; the better form of what those "broke folks"
should be if they just worked harder.

Evidently, that was not an environment that I thrived in. There's the adage of not allowing money to
change you. I've made the most money of anyone in my immediate family and that of my extended and
that didn't turn me into a middle class bag chaser. In fact, it made me question how is it that I
can be paid to attend virtual meetings and sling code in California for twice of the salary of an
experienced emergency room nurse[^4]. It's easy to blame capitalism but I'm coming to understand
that it's not just capitalism that dictates how we determine worth.

## Society and Information Literacy
I want to make one thing clear: it's evident to me that you can spend close to a decade in academia,
in some of the best schools in the country, if not the world, and still come out more uneducated and
uninformed about what moves the world. I focus on MIT in this example, after reading [Black
Software][5], due to its history in working to produce workers and "leaders of industry"[^5] that
work to continue the imperialist agenda of the United States in every vertical. We don't call it
that, in my industry. It's described as "getting the bag", "building generational wealth" or other
things that tend to mimic the systems that justify all sorts of violence &mdash; emotional,
psychological, fiscal, political &mdash; you name it. There's this idea that if we replace the folks
in charge of a system, that, in itself, is enough to bring about change. 2008 has shown this to be
false for the executive branch of the US government and for New York City's current mayor so we
might need to revisit [what Ruha Benjamin has mentioned][6] with the cosplay of change.
