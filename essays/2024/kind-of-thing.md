As per usual, someone mentioned that Aral's written a catch-up of their perspective on both how the Fedora/IBM
situation on accessibility is going down[^1]. It's an excellent catch-up on the events from the top (to a point), which I've kept an
eye on. Before we continue, I want to make some points clear:

1. **Accessibility is _a critical_ component of computing**. I've made that point clear in my initial responses to Aral that
   have _remained_ ignored. In fact, I initially _stayed out_ because I knew how vocal of an advocate Aral's been with
   regards to this. What pulled me in [was a comment questioning][1] if Aral's doing this as a stance of virtue signaling.
2. **Whiteness is not tied to nationality, ethnicity or nationality**. I made this point clear to Aral with a link with
   information about it, a concept contributed to by the people he has/had pinned on his profile page like Angela Davis
   and held up by Palestinian scholars like Edward Said. This has gone completely ignored.
3. **Those who have the privilege of contribution need to utilize it.** The person who highlighted Aral's response to me
   noted that F/LOSS (correctly so) has a culture of "patches welcome" towards people who bring up concerns. This is a
   well documented phenomenon and a side-effect of the culture _of privilege_ (be it explicit in readjusting one's
   assets to work on it full-time or implicit in working in industry and accruing _a lot_ of disposable income). Quite a
   few people have written on this concept[^2].

That said, I feel like I have to provide some points of reflection. It's overly clear that the space of tech, doubly so
with the F/LOSS communities, that the concept of identity politics[^3] is frayed. I have to repeat this once more, _your
nationality does not dictate society's perception of your race_. I linked the Wikipedia page on the concept and will ask
for more focus to [the sub-theory of inflation][2]. I'm not going to completely dive into this[^4] because it'll distract from
the core point I made in [my last entry](./oh-aral/). I want to focus more on the victim complex that Aral's managed to
conjure here then return to the point of pseudo-advocacy.

I took the time to reply to each point he made in the initial comment. All but one went ignored. There's no mention of _concrete_ reflection 
on this in his later posts (either on Mastodon on his blog). In fact, the focus he made towards the end of his blog post of _the trivialization of global 
identity_ and how that plays into online (and offline) political discourse confirmed to me something explained by Cheryl I. Harris in [Whiteness as
Property](https://harvardlawreview.org/print/no-volume/whiteness-as-property/). She writes:

> The persistence of passing is related to the historical and continuing pattern of white racial domination and economic
> exploitation, which has invested passing with a certain economic logic. [...] Becoming white meant gaining access to a
> whole set of public and private privileges that materially and _permanently_ guaranteed basic s subsistence needs and,
> therefore, survival. Becoming white increased the possibility of controlling critical aspects of one's life rather than
> being the object of others' domination.

We can continue showing how European settlers even perceived property as both fungible and a tool of power:

> Whiteness fits the broad historical concept of property described by classical theorists. In James Madison's view, for
> example, property "embraces every thing to which a man may attach a value and have a right", referring to all of a
> person's legal rights.

It goes on:

> Property as conceived in the founding era included not only external objects and people's relationships to them, but
> also all of those human rights, liberties, powers, and immunities that are important for human well-being, including
> freedom of expression, freedom of conscience, freedom from bodily harm and free and equal opportunities to use
> personal facilities.
