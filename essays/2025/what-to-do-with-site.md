This isn't going to be short. I need to weave some things together to explain
why I'm taking the semi-drastic approach I outline at the end.

---

Some time ago, I mentioned that I wanted to [refashion my site in a way that
mirrored the things][1] I _want_ to talk and share about on the public Internet.
I've had some hesitations on this nowadays, since non-consensual commercial
Internet scraping has _really soured_ my want to continue adding things to
their virtual fishing net of content. Their end goal is to _automate_ the act
of being connected to one another (if not make it _extremely_ difficult to
figure out the difference between a person or machine); something to be made
into a premium experience[^1]; if not one that has folks moving into more
private spaces like Discord (or nowhere at all). This has been made more clear
with things like Sora and Zuckerberg's persistent want to bring more folks into
the Metaverse.

## The Butterfly in the Room

Frankly, folks logging off and looking for _offline_ spaces to organize and
connect might be more of a benefit than relying on publicly monitored online
systems. The idea of a "open forum" wasn't consistently a truth[^2] and was
reduced when those who had discomfort with the "virtue signaling" of folks
became almost unavoidable. Ironically, Jay Graber, [with their interview with
WIRED][3] and that with [The New Yorker][4], about her nascent-now-30-million
strong social network that started as a brainchild at Twitter itself, once ran
under an umbrella of communication freedom. The project attempting to divorce
itself from the idea that politics has no bearing on both how communication
works and can be done online has led to scrutiny by vocal folks on the
platform[^3]. Interestingly enough, history has shown that the means of
communication and whose who have the means to do so has _routinely_ been
political and will find itself at odds (or agreement) with the State in which
it resides. The question is less about what features or upload limits they'll
provide but how _aligned_ in action and mantra will they be to these activites.
There's a striking difference between Graber's outward stances and that of
[Meredith Whittaker][mw], for example[^5].

Despite the issues with ActivityPub, it does have an approach of extensibility
that mirrors another underspoken protocol, XMPP, with its Federation Extension
Proposals (FEPs), that allow folks to advertise what features they support.
This allows for a bit more rapid and experimental development than what ATProto
provides but due to a different level of interest and speed in the
ActivityPub-centric Fediverse[^4], we might not see things like [full-on OAuth2
profiles][6] in mainline applications made more common; something that's
resulted in a lot more development in the ATmosphere.

Bluesky is easy for me to focus on as it's where I spend more of my
microblogging time (I peek at my timelines on Mastodon but rarely engage;
there's been less of an incentive to). Instagram's where I tend to stay in
touch with folks I've known since small &mdash; too young for that to be the
reason I stick on Facebook but too old not to still have an account. All in all
&mdash; it's a very fractured life of social networking that reminds me that
most of the talk and ambition of the open social Web remains in a lucid dream. The
dream is that we can casually convince folks to move away from systems that have
spent millions of speculative and compounded capital to keep folks scrolling,
chatting and sending things on their platforms: the real enclosure of the
public square.

A lot of words to say that leaning into a _specific_ protocol can immediately
become risky, despite notions of [their necessity][7], the makers and stewards
of the platforms (and/or protocols) need to be aligned with the people who end
up using them; especially the most vulnerable (or prone to attack) of those
platforms. Without that, we create _new_ holes\_ of harm for these folks.
