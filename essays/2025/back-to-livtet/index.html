Focusing on Livtet Yet Again

by Jacky Alciné • published March 14, 2025
As I look for work, please support me with a donation to the project development I'm spending in the Atmosphere — donate here

In order to pull myself away from the more time consuming parts of the Internet, I've decided to return my attention to Livtet. For those who don't know what it is, it's a local-first book management application that borne out of my need to organize my home collection. This time, I decided to get less galaxy-brained about supporting a bunch of stuff that took more time for me to implement and get to the guts of what I really needed for myself: a way to manage and annotate books that I have in my possession[1]. I wrote about the initial ambitions with it last year and got too focused on the infrastructure of something that didn't live yet. Anyway, to progress.

Resetting Objectives

One thing that immediately plagued me was the interface. I fell back into my common "dev as a startup" stance, which also involved pulling out a book to skim chapters on design. With some refreshed guidance and a target, I had a better sense of what to run along with. The design aspect of a home library system isn't too novel (and frankly homogeneous), which made me a bit comfortable to keep things more simpler than I expected. I spent more time reworking the page for showing the book itself to allow myself to work backwards from what I want this thing to do for me:

It's still a work in progress; I'm wrestling on how I want to present the audit log of changes below. I still have a strong want to make this part of a set of applications:

  • one that runs on my laptop or PC that I can use for longer sessions of cataloguing and general backups,
  • one on my phone that I can use for updating this information when I'm on the go, not on my laptop or the like
  • via an extension for KOReader so I can get my notes, progress and the like all backed up and easy to search elsewhere

For now, I'm focusing on getting the desktop application to a place where I can come to it weekly and have it do what I expect. I'm eager to write more about it and open to ideas on how to present information. There is a goal to make a Web server that one could self-host to serve some bits of information from the database; something like Calibre-Web but more ... sociably inclined.

  1. It does include books I borrow from friends or the library — that tends to be a reliable "loan to own" path for me. I've bought books I loved after reading them at the library or if a friend had loaned it to me for a period of time. ↩︎
