Ghost is demonstrating the power of independent media being able to fight in a
landscape of venture capital capture and corporate consolidation. The shape of
the organization running Ghost is a non-profit, which is more common for open
source projects to take on; another example of the lack of the need to run to
the explicit for-profit model to make change.

The excuses I tend to see about the "lock-in" [of using Substack][1] lean on
the very things that Ghost provides &mdash; even before this release. This has
me believing that, in some cases, publishers &mdash; be it large or small
&mdash; don't _mind cohabiting with Nazi content_. [The access they gain in
these circles][3] seems to more important than the integrity of what they report
on. I've seen a few publishers, namely Taylor Lorenz, call this call to leave
Substack a form of virtue signaling, from those who are in her comments about
it[^1]. I have a bit of a qualm with the concept of [digital virtue
signaling][2] but I can see how folks who aren't funding you but wanting to
give you executive direction as a way of "yelling from outside of the club".

Looks like there's less reason to stick in the NaziStack, though!
