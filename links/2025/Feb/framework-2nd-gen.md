The CEO opening with a statement that "we're the only company that can announce a product and it
look the same each year with applause" made me smile but Apple has definitely locked in that title.
To their credit, as a Framework 13" owner, these machines are _built_ such that you won't have to
keep replacing it over and over. Because of things I'm working on the side, I immediately
noticed a few mentions of companies in the hardware. Namely, Honeywell; a household brand that keeps
you cool and also working on [literal _energy_ weapons][1] &mdash; once sci-fi shit, but now they're
in the new Framework 13 boards to keep them cool. Cool?

They mentioned that the stock shipping versions will come with a Microsoft CoPilot button and the
DIY versions will be without. I understand that Microsoft has the weight of manufacturing control
when it comes to this but I do wish that, in a different world, more PC makers would push back
against Microsoft in _some_ capacity.

## Curiosity and Ambition
<figure>
    <img src="/images/links/2025-Feb-framework-pc.png"
        alt="A screenshot of the CEO standing with the Framework Desktop in view" />
</figure>

I was extremely eager to see the new desktop release by Framework. I have no need for such a device
&mdash; I already own enough machines reflective of my class in society. The novelity of a small PC, especially
one small enough to port around is attractive &mdash; as someone who needs to have a separate
machine when doing presentations or tech work in the community, this would help out quite a bit.

<figure>
    <img src="/images/links/2025-Feb-framework-ai.png"
        alt="A screenshot of the AI specification of the AMD base board" />
</figure>

The "AI" advertising is lazy. This is not Framework's fault but it's potentially clear that
AMD is pushing hard to get more "AI"-centric cards into the world, perhaps to challenge NVIDIA (in
some capacity). And it seems like this is the only approach for boards available as all of them are
branded "AI". In a different world, I'd be more interested in a more _generic_ board (which doesn't
feel too impossible &mdash; look at the [laptop board offerings][9]).

Marketing language aside, it's clear that this is something that they can still push to tinkerers
since the casing for the PC is relatively standard: with [their experimental RISC-V board][10], I'd be
eager to see them consider developing an equivalent to allow for truly open computing - from the
silicion to the software.

> Tangentially, I do loathe the adoption of the "elevated keynote" thing that the tech industry
> adopted from Apple. I can't give Apple the full share of blame &mdash; Microsoft and IBM did this
> in the past. At the end of the day, they all want you to buy &mdash; regardless of need.

<figure>
    <img src="/images/links/2025-Feb-framework-12.jpg"
        alt="An CGI illustration of a 12' Framework with its stylus and repairment tool" />
</figure>

The 2-in-1 12" laptop is a _really_ interesting product offering. It's extremely niche of a idea
&mdash; and it's definitely something that can help folks escape the Apple ecosystem if they're
aggressively locked into the portable computing landscape. I would *love* this as my introduction
machine; I have a now-defunct Dell XPS 2-in-1 that got a lot of use in its tent and tablet mode for
reading and watching media.

## Things I'm Interested In
<figure>
    <img src="/images/links/2025-Feb-framework-bezel.png"
        alt="A screenshot of the CEO holding a Framework 13' with a translucent purple bezel" />
</figure>

I'm personally considering getting the following:

* The [purple translucent bezel][2] for the Framework 13": I already have the solid one and my
  machine's dominantely this color so it feels apt.
* [An Ethernet expansion card][3] for personal projects around wired systems.
* [A 32GB RAM stick][4]; I've been great with a solo 32GB for virtualization work but I'd like to give
  my host machine more breathing room as I work on things, like media editing and game development.
* [A bigger battery][5] because my battery life over the last two years has taken a nose dive. I do
  use this as my primary machine and I rarely shut it down (it's usually in suspension, docked or
  completely off when I travel).
* [The 2.8k display][6] also feels like a necessary bump with the media and game work I'm doing as
  well.
* [A hat][7] because my hair's finally short enough that I can wear caps and I like the logo.

Some of these parts will lead to me having spares, which the community tends to have plenty of. When
I do retire this PCB, if not because of wear, I'll turn it into a HDTV receiver or donate compute
cycles for some sort of [open computing project][8].
