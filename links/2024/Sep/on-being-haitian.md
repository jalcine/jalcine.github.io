Jose Vilson's reflection on the role of how education, as a marker of status, of identity and of
self-direction gave me a bit to think about. I'm not the _popular_ type of Haitian; I don't folllow
any organized religions, I don't have any form of heavy accrediations and I don't optimize myself
around making a lot of money. For many migrants and children thereof, there is a pressure to
assimiliate heavily into what conservative Black America deems as the "way forward": buying our way
to respect. However, Vilson breaks away from that a bit here with the focus on education: one of the
career paths that's routinely discouraged because it's not as profitable as being a doctor or
lawyer[^1].

His reference to teachers being the vanguard of society is something I resonate with and it comes as
the antithesis of the aforementioned: instead of an obsession with building a golden calf today, we
work to ensure more stable and fertile lands for the future. It's not to fall in the path of
longtermism but to understand what things we are doing today that we have seen yesterday that'll
make tomorrow more challenging for those who are going to be in our positions.

What his cousin said is something I'm considering heavily, provided life and resources align; on the
idea that school can be a place to return if one is seeking guidance or feeling lost in life.
Perhaps.

[^1]: The exception to teaching in this capacity would be if you were a high-ranking (thus more
    highly paid) official within academia. It's less about what you do for the children and more
    aobut how much you get paid.
