One thing I find about authors; good ones, is that they can give a good talk. Doctorow's one of
them. While I have gripes with his work on [Chokepoint Capitalism][1], it'd be disrespectful to
ignore the research and accessibility of the book itself. This talk opens up with a quick retelling
and explaining as to how finance began taking control of then-utility-centric companies became more
degraded experiences over time.

People have mentioned that he's a good orator and I see why &mdash; again, I attribute this to being
a good writer. He swiftly highlights the coupling of state and corporations working to milk every
ounce of value from people and manufacturing even more value by crafting _more_ value from
commodifying every aspect of our lives. As someone, for a very brief moment, who played into the
idea of the [quantitative self][2], this was something I bought into. It wasn't an explicitly
selling of myself but it was a public _validation_ of the mass quantification of behavior that we
see companies now sell as a new flagship feature, as opposed to making things like replaceable
batteries or alternative application distribution channels.

[Cory's video from the year prior][3] is also good and it's clear that he picked up exactly where he
left off.
