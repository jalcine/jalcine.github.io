Anil writes briefly about the nature of billionaires and tech venture capitalists from his
experience around them. Folks who seem to imagine themselves as the new Rockfellers and the ilk,
with near-full control over industry and policy when it comes to tech. Tech, both as a concept and
an identity (to some), effectively moves to their whim. He concludes mentioning his faith in those
working outside of this insular realm to help counteract their actions.

I'd believe one of those forms of action to be in the form of collective organizing: where workers
recognize and pool their labor-power to both reign in power and reclaim it from those who control
our ability to support ourselves. However, it's important to peek a bit closer at what this means.
The once-glamor child of the tech industry and social networking space, Jack Dorsey, has taken a
pretty hard turn towards the right. He's gone from attempting to help foster the open social Web with some
initial funding for BlueSky but now has become the silent and invisible contender in the space of
cryptocurrency work under the moniker, [TBD][1]. Quite annoying to search. I do believe that
_solely_ focusing on these people, the VC QAnon, is doing the same amount of unncessary narrowing of
focus as we saw with Donald Trump and focusing _solely_ on him as the source of American issues.

We need to be more honest and broaden our understanding of _why_ this industry is so easily capable
of producing people like this. It's not because money corrupts. One of the reasons is that it's
extremely profitable to work _against_ the needs of people. A prime example of this shows up in
[Malcolm Harris][2]' [Palo Alto][3]; a working history in the development of the "Silicon Valley" as
he recounts how tech is yet the expansion of how this country _demands_ production at the cost of
people here and abroad. As Netscape grew, computer manufactures worked with state governments for
fast track access to handle the fabrication of the personal computers and ship it back to the United
States at a premium to citizens. This is now the model used by _every_ company: from Apple to
Samsung, as described in [Dying for an iPhone][4].

Yes, these former nerds now billionaires are on one _and_ the systems we use everyday that we are
committed to upholding further entrench their power. That's as long as we allow it to. Reclaiming
control of our lives can take on many forms, from labor organizing to progressive lobbying to direct
action. But we can't keep it as just the off-shoot of a few folks wanting control over everyone
else. We also live on this planet &mdash; on this Web.
