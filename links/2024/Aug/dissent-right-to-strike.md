This piece by [Veena Dubal][1] for Dissent has been the _longest_ read in this issue for me. So many
moments where they've expanded on how, even despite Justice Jackson's work to highlight how striking
is necessary for workers in a democratic society, the majority opinion is increasinlgy swaying
towards corporate control. They include a quote from Sean O'Brien after [the case of Glacier vs
Teamsters][2] that "people cannot rely on their governments or their courts to protect them ... We
must rely on each other". This, despite being nearly a year old, is a good call to action for those
interested or adjacent to organizing to understand that we, in the United States, are fighting a
system that is inherently _against_ workers at every turn.

As mentioned, wildcat strikes[^1] are our strongest weapon. They demonstrate a truer form of
workplace solidarity that'll be needed regardless of who takes office in January 2025. It's humbling
to know that even despite [the anti-worker and anti-Black presidental campaign][3] (nee administration) of
the 1980s and its push to reduce worker organizing, there's been growth. "According to the Economic
Policy Institute, U.S. strike activity increased by nearly 50 percent last year &mdash; even as the
percentage of workers represented by a union dropped one-third of a percentage point", writes Dubal.

If you're a worker, _especially_ if you're in tech, the best time to get organized was yesterday.
The next best is today. If you're able to come to San Francisco in October, there's a conference
[_just for you_][4].
