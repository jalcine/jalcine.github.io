Erin Kissane is launching "Cloud Atlas" &mdash; an interview-based guide to the
apps and projects emerging on the AT Protocol/Atmosphere, intended to make
decentralized social media infrastructure _more legible_ to people interested
in alternatives to centralized platforms. This is something I haven't seen done
from _the outside_ &mdash; developers of these apps and services tend to be
their own marketing departments and PR teams but this isn't (solely) _that_.

> The human texture and qualitative specificity of projects in the Atmosphere
> are as interesting to me as any set of technical and design specifications
> could possibly be. Interesting for their own sake, and also for the ways
> they’re shaping each project’s current state and its likely futures. I also
> believe in just getting out there and gathering foundational knowledge.
> There's value in offering classification systems even when they're
> provisional and even when they’re only one among many. I read a lot of oral
> histories and interviews and human-textured research from older social
> movements and periods in technology that inflects my understanding of the
> world now—the ACT UP oral history project that kept me sane in during the
> Covid Tracking project, the very human testimonies of the Combahee River
> Collective, even the (delightful) work that came out of the early stumbles of
> the Worm Community System. I like to think that if things go even halfway
> well, the work we’re all doing to make new online social systems legible will
> help in the present and be a useful ship’s log for the people who come after
> us. Understanding what we’re trying do—and why—will matter later, is the bet.

This is the kind of work that keeps projects both alive _and_ afloat in the
minds of many. I'm extremely curious to see how Erin pulls this off and kind of
hoping that my own project, [Livtet][1] gets to be in this party.
