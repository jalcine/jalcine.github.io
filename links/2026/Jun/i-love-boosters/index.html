I Love Boosters

by Wikipedia — bookmarked June 16, 2026

I enjoyed every moment of I Love Boosters. From Lakeith Stanfield's attempt to woo our lead character to the literal heightening of contradiction that led to a global workers' revolt — this is the kind of media that makes plain the difficulty of the people who are ignored and belabored for the comfort of the upper class. It focused on the production of fast fashion, the industry that's the underacknowledged child of the industralization of attire and the elitism of what it means to be "in style" (Haute couture, for example).

I'm glad that they took time to explain something every baby Marxist (or serious leftist, that is) has to understand: dialectical materialism. The most ridiculous moment of this is quite literally a scene of .. well, sex but second to that was a moment when Taylour Paige's character was blasted by the "situational accelerator" that works as a way to exacerbate conflict of a situation or to deconstruct every element of material that produces the object. One example of this was the aforementioned sex scene - which was very abrupt - but also her donning a suit that heightened her senses:

This movie is one I'm adding to my personal DVD collection and will throw on when I want something to remind me that you can make a funny, zany and educational film for the left all in one go.
