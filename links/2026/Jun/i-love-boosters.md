I enjoyed every moment of [I Love Boosters][1]. From Lakeith Stanfield's
attempt to woo our lead character to the _literal_ heightening of contradiction
that led to a global workers' revolt &mdash; _this_ is the kind of media that
makes plain the difficulty of the people who are ignored and belabored for the
comfort of the upper class. It focused on the production of _fast fashion_, the
industry that's the underacknowledged child of the industralization of attire
and the elitism of what it means to be "in style" ([Haute couture][2], for
example).

I'm glad that they took time to explain something every baby Marxist
(or serious leftist, that is) _has_ to understand: [dialectical
materialism][3]. The most ridiculous moment of this is quite literally a scene
of .. well, sex but second to that was a moment when Taylour Paige's character
was blasted by the "situational accelerator" that works as a way to exacerbate
conflict of a situation or to deconstruct every element of material that
produces the object. One example of this was the aforementioned sex scene -
which was very abrupt - but also her donning a suit that heightened her senses:

![Taylour in a body suit](/images/links/taylor-suit.jpg)

This movie is one I'm adding to my personal DVD collection and will throw on
when I want something to remind me that you can make a funny, zany and
educational film for the left all in one go.
